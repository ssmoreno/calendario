# SS Calendar design

SS Calendar is a quiet, full-page workspace with two areas: the upcoming Google Calendar events and a profile menu. The Agent is summoned rather than sited. There is no calendar grid or event editor.

The palette is Pumpkin Spice over Ink Black, with Stone Brown carrying secondary text. Dark is the default and the signature; a warm cream canvas is the alternate. The accent is bright enough to fill but not to letter, so `--accent` paints surfaces, dots, and indicators, `--accent-ink` is the text-and-focus-ring variant, and every accent fill pairs its label with `--on-accent`. Behind the sheet the canvas is two fixed layers: a pair of pumpkin blooms with an edge vignette, and fine grain that keeps the large flat fields from banding. Each event carries one of four hues, which a block spends as its left edge and its tint — the app accent stays reserved for the app's own chrome and for the one block that is next.

A slim top bar carries the wordmark and its accent dot, the Agent's summoning button, a connection pill, and the profile menu. Below it, one rounded sheet holds the board and nothing else: columns of event blocks, with days acting as the dividers inside them. The board is packed, not scrolled. It looks fifteen days ahead and draws as many as fit, flowing each day down a column and then across to the next so that reading left-to-right, top-to-bottom stays chronological. Three columns is the resting shape; the board widens to at most seven only to avoid cutting days off, and never past what the width can carry. A day is never split across columns, a day too busy for one column keeps that column to itself, and whatever is left over is named in a single quiet line rather than silently dropped. Blocks are a fixed height, because the packing budgets for them; details open in a popover instead of expanding in place, which would shove the days behind them into other columns. The sheet also absorbs the loading, disconnected, authorization, and service-error states, centred where the board would be.

The Agent has one presence on the page: a button in the top bar, and `⌘K`. Pressing it opens a Spotlight bar — a centred pill over a dimmed canvas, holding nothing but the composer. The first message turns that bar into the conversation: the same surface travels to the right edge of the screen and grows into a docked panel, floating over a board that does not move underneath it. It is one fixed-position element in every state, so the journey is a plain transition between two sets of lengths. Closing fades it where it stands. It stays mounted while shut, so a turn already in flight still lands, and the button carries a pulse while it does.

On narrow screens the docked panel becomes a sheet off the bottom and the board falls to fewer columns. Settings is the same sheet as a stack of rows: what a setting is on the left, how to change it on the right.

Every action in the app is one control with four variants — primary, secondary, quiet, icon — so heights, radii, and states cannot drift between pages. Type, spacing, radius, and easing all resolve through tokens in `globals.css`; the radii are sized so a nested shape lands back on the scale. Keyboard focus uses the shared accent outline, the closed Agent layer is `inert` so it contributes nothing to the focus order, and reduced-motion preferences disable nonessential transitions. Manrope carries the UI, Geist Mono carries temporal metadata and the uppercase eyebrow labels that head every section.
